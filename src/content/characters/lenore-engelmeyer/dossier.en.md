## Drevholt and the Voren name

Drevholt is an inland town east of Vel’hara. Members of the Voren branch settled there after the destruction of the old Voren Works in IY 1043. The branch retained the Engelmeyer name while its place in the House’s succession lay dormant. Re’lha carried the Voren name. Al’tair, a physician who had come to Drevholt, adopted it on marrying her. Their daughter was registered at birth as Le’nore Engelmeyer. The full form lived in the branch’s records and in the family’s understanding of where it came from.

As a girl, Le’nore read early and pursued a question long after other people had lost interest in it. Al’tair kept careful notes and took incomplete answers seriously. She learned the value of that attention in a small town that had few words for some of the things its families lived with. In company she often preferred to listen first. Her warmth, when she chose to come forward, readily put people at ease.

## The market incidents and Academy assessment

Around IY 1564, three older adolescents confronted Le’nore in Drevholt’s market district. Witnesses recorded simultaneous disorientation and a loss of awareness of her presence. The three recovered physically unharmed after she had walked away. A similar incident the following year accelerated an assessment by the Imperial Redactor Academy at Hora’veil.

The first assessor recorded a genuine Dive signature unusually quiet for the strength of the effect. A specialist panel reached the Deception classification after examining how the effects fit the established Anchor domains. The Academy certified Le’nore as a Quill around IY 1566 and issued Mark 2 for practice within defined limits. The classification is confirmed in the active registry.

## How the registered practice works

A perceptual change can be as small as ceasing to notice somebody who is still in the room. During the Dive, Le’nore uses Deception to redirect sensory information as it reaches the person who would perceive it. One channel can be affected while others carry on normally. The effect can therefore escape casual notice even when the underlying Redactory disturbance is detectable by a sensitive Point or prepared harmonic array.

Precision and scope depend on usable Depth and Reach. The practice places demands on the Redactor and remains subject to Overreach. During civilian work, the registry requires a practitioner to disclose operational perceptual influence on people who have given no consent. Le’nore also attends scheduled Depth assessments at Hora’veil. The Mark covers civic practice. House standing carries no additional authority over her licence.

## Reintegration and present jurisdiction

The death of the previous blood Seat in IY 1565 left House Engelmeyer without a direct eligible heir. The resulting succession proceedings reopened the question of the Voren branch’s standing. They concluded in IY 1567 with formal recognition of Le’nore’s lineage name and her place as the House’s blood Seat. The Furnace Council retained day-to-day administration. For Le’nore, recognition also made it possible to ask questions about a family history the main line had rarely been willing to discuss.

The House arranged her move to Vel’hara that year. The city-state is independent. Its inter-registry compact allows the Academy at Hora’veil to maintain the imperial side of her practice file with Vel’haran consent. Her work there remains independent and civic.

## Family and the life she is making

Al’tair now lives in Vel’hara as well. They have kept up a correspondence since Drevholt and have yet to meet in the city. Her letters are affectionate and carefully measured. The proximity matters to a woman accustomed to following every available lead. The first conversation in years is one she has allowed to remain in the future.

The House offers recognition and a degree of autonomy. Its main line also has expectations of the person who holds its Seat. Le’nore is learning which of those expectations she can live with while taking on civic work on her own terms.
