Medical Redactory can keep a person alive, restore damaged tissue, create temporary function, or help a body complete a repair it could not reach alone. Those outcomes can look identical during treatment. They are not structurally identical after release.

The field's governing question is:

> **When the route releases, what carries the next moment?**

If the answer is still “the Redactory route,” the result is **instanced support**. If ordinary body structure, metabolism, circulation, material supply, and self-maintaining process carry it onward, the result has achieved **causal closure**.

## Real Support, Temporary Continuation

An instanced organ, airway, vessel wall, tissue bridge, or pain-regulation pathway is real and functional while the route holds. “Temporary” does not mean imaginary. In an emergency, such support may create the time in which surgery, medicine, natural healing, or stable reconfiguration becomes possible.

But once the route releases, an instanced structure disappears. If nothing has changed beneath it, the original injury remains. A treatment plan that forgets this distinction can turn a successful rescue into a delayed collapse.

Long-term instanced support may be ethical and necessary when no durable alternative exists. It should be named honestly as accommodation or chronic support, with recovery schedules, apparatus redundancy, and [[route-burn-in|burn-in]] risk treated as part of the care rather than hidden as technical detail.

## What Stable Healing Requires

A durable body cannot be made from instruction alone. Stable healing must resolve:

- usable biological substrate;
- energy and metabolic demand;
- heat;
- waste and displaced material;
- circulation, innervation, and integration with existing tissue;
- a viable process by which the body continues the result.

Living bodies help because they already possess repair, regulation, and material exchange. Redactory can route those processes, supply prepared feedstock, hold alignment, or open a configuration the body can then maintain. It cannot simply ignore mass, energy, toxicity, rejection, or the consequences of forcing growth too quickly.

The same rule governs nonliving repair. A restored bridge, tool, or wall must have material, load paths, and a stable post-release structure. Appearance alone is not repair.

## Treating Another Person

Healing oneself and healing another person are structurally different acts. A patient is another recursive singularity with their own self-address and [[syntax-authority|recursive sovereignty]]. Their body does not become the clinician's instrument merely because treatment would help.

The patient's ongoing self-model creates resistance to outside alteration. Consent reduces that conflict and makes cooperation possible, but it does not fuse clinician and patient or remove the need for technical fit. Emergency authority can justify the minimum necessary intervention when meaningful consent is unavailable; it does not create general ownership of the patient's body.

Medical schematics therefore define not just a desired anatomy, but permissible boundaries, monitoring, feedstock, release criteria, reversal options, and the point at which ordinary clinicians must take over.

## Route-Breaking Care

When medical support has burned into the practitioner who supplies it—or into a Redactor-patient maintaining their own function—removing the apparatus is not treatment. Suppression can stop visible routing while leaving the incorporated route ready to reopen.

Route-breaking requires a participating Redactor, a replacement plan for the supported function, and patient choice about the risks of release. VÆstar care combines ordinary medicine, apparatus control, continuity monitoring, and Redactory expertise because no one discipline can safely answer all four questions.

## Limits

Medical Redactory does not make scarcity disappear. Skilled practitioners, compatible Anchors, safe Leaves, feedstock, sterile facilities, heat disposal, recovery time, and long-term care all remain limited. It also does not guarantee that every former bodily state is still reachable or that restoration to an earlier appearance would be the patient's chosen good.

The best medical work is not the most spectacular transformation. It is the result that remains true after the Redactor lets go.
