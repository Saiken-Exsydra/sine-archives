SOE-001 is the case that taught [[stasis|StaSis]] that information containment can be physical containment.

Called **That Which Gives Chase**, it is not made dangerous merely by a name, image, or isolated fact. The hazard emerges through **accumulated relation**: observations that are individually survivable can become an address when assembled into a sufficiently coherent model.

For that reason, no public entry—and no single internal office—contains a complete account.

## Controlled Incompleteness

StaSis handles SOE-001 through deliberate separation:

- observations are divided among custodians;
- identifiers and event indices are kept apart;
- systems capable of automatic synthesis are restricted;
- staff receive only the context required for a specific task;
- apparent duplication is preserved when removing it would reveal a hidden relation.

This is not ordinary secrecy. The goal is not simply to prevent people from learning an embarrassing fact. The goal is to prevent an observing system—human, institutional, or technological—from becoming complete enough to function as a route.

The resulting archive can look inefficient by design. Gaps, incompatible labels, separated ledgers, and manual transfers are safety features.

## The Year 900 Crisis

The principal recorded crisis occurred around Year 900, when advances in record correlation allowed scattered reports to be treated as one event-set. The response broke that synthesis before the relation could stabilize. It did not prove SOE-001 dead, defeated, or permanently excluded.

Later histories sometimes describe the event as a victory over a pursuing entity. StaSis doctrine is more cautious: the institution survived a moment in which its own knowledge had become dangerously navigable.

The crisis helped produce the **Ledger Famine**, a period of destroyed, divided, degraded, or deliberately unlinked records. The loss impaired ordinary governance and research for generations, but it also prevented later systems from inheriting a ready-made address.

## What Does Not Work

Force can interrupt a manifestation without resolving the relation that permitted it. A Redactory effect can alter a local condition without erasing information already distributed elsewhere. A continuity lock can hold an event-set in temporary self-agreement, but a cleaner, more unified record may increase the very danger it is meant to contain.

The common demand to “put everything in one secure archive” is therefore specifically unsafe for SOE-001.

## Standing Public Doctrine

Personnel encountering a possible SOE-001 relation are instructed not to compare unassigned records, not to create a master timeline, and not to feed fragmented evidence into general synthesis systems. A gap should not be filled merely because it is visible.

SOE-001 remains uncontained in the conventional sense. Its containment is a civilization continuing to remember **less than it could assemble**.
