## Overview

Halk'en Orveth is a centuries-old active priest in Obsidian, the Church's specialist arm for possession, Divine Vessels, dangerous spiritual manifestations, and the situations ordinary clergy are not expected to survive. He has served for more than three and a half centuries. Most people who know his name know the same handful of facts: he is a Rector-Ascendant, he carries an ancient sword called **Sundersaint**, and when his divine power becomes visible, red hands appear behind him.

There can be as many as seven.

The obvious reading is that Halk'en is a man built for command. He is physically imposing, speaks with enormous ecclesiastical authority, has spent centuries entering rooms where hesitation kills people, and carries a manifestation capable of restraining possessed bodies, reinforcing other priests, forcing hostile presences to answer, and—at its highest known expression—making a contested situation behave as though jurisdiction itself has been settled.

The correction is more important than the reputation.

Halk'en is profoundly suspicious of command.

He does not touch people casually. He asks permission before laying a hand on someone when ritual does not already require it. He removes his gauntlets before blessing children. He dislikes ornate symbols of office. He has refused advancement, argued against forced religious investiture, and once summarized his own theology with the claim that authority becomes legitimate only when the person exercising it accepts responsibility for what follows.

That restraint is not an aesthetic quirk. It is the discipline around which his entire life has been built.

Halk'en's gift concerns authority so directly that every success contains the seed of the same danger: if command works, if people survive because he acts, and if institutions repeatedly call him only when everyone else has failed, it becomes very easy to begin treating the ability to impose order as proof that order should be imposed.

He has spent centuries trying not to become that man.

Whether centuries of success have made the effort easier or harder is the question beneath almost everything he does.

## Personality and Presence

Halk'en is formal, patient, direct, and much gentler than his battlefield reputation suggests.

He speaks slowly because he dislikes wasting words, especially words that may be heard as instruction. He almost never raises his voice. Field witnesses repeatedly note the opposite pattern: when a situation turns violent, his voice tends to become softer.

He is kind, but not always comforting.

Halk'en does not confuse compassion with permission to avoid reality. He can sit with the frightened, bless the dying, repair an old parish door with his own hands, remember the name of someone he saved a century earlier, or spend time correcting the spelling of a dead soldier on a memorial because the mistake should not have survived the man. His tenderness is practical. It tends to become something done.

He is also capable of placing frightening demands on people he believes are able to carry them.

This is where his virtues become dangerous without turning false. Halk'en respects responsibility so deeply that he has difficulty understanding people who step away from burdens he believes remain theirs. He has contempt for nobles who treat inherited power as decoration, clergy who use office as shelter, fighters who enjoy being feared, and capable people who pretend helplessness because helplessness would excuse them from obligation.

The word **capable** does an alarming amount of work in his moral reasoning.

He knows this.

His stabilizing habits are unusually deliberate: regular confession, acceptance of medical and ecclesiastical monitoring, low tolerance for flattery, willingness to accept correction from subordinates on technical matters, and an explicit fear of enjoying command for its own sake. He has asked the Church to ensure that people around him are willing to stop him if that fear ever becomes justified.

He does not consider himself immune to corruption simply because he is afraid of it.

That may be the healthiest thing about him.

His relationship with touch reflects the same discipline. Halk'en's divine manifestation expresses itself as hands—hands that authorize, witness, restrain, invest, judge, command, and impose dominion. He therefore treats his ordinary human hands almost ceremonially. A handshake is not meaningless to him. A hand on someone's shoulder is not automatically harmless. The man who can make touch binding refuses to pretend touch has no moral weight when the miracle is absent.

Strangers sometimes mistake this care for coldness or awkwardness.

It is neither.

Halk'en knows exactly what a hand can do.

## Background

Halk'en was born c. Imperial Year 2025 in **Black Mere**, a cold village-parish in the Northern Region of the Empire. His father, Maren Orveth, worked as a ferryman and lay cantor. His mother, Selka Orveth, was a parish copyist. The surviving record contains no House lineage, noble inheritance, Redactory dynasty, or political connection waiting to explain what he became.

His childhood reputation was built less on holiness than on the Northern habit of doing the necessary thing before anyone has time to name it a virtue.

At twelve, he pulled another child from a flooded millrace. At fourteen, he beat a possessed horse back with a shovel while it was trampling a priest. At fifteen, he carried an injured priest through the snow to a hospice and lost full mobility in three frostbitten toes for the trouble.

The priest lived.

Halk'en kept the stiffness in his right foot and later refused several opportunities to have it corrected.

He entered minor seminary at sixteen. The records describe a large, quiet student with excellent memory, strong liturgical discipline, little interest in decorative theology, and a persistent habit of translating doctrine into practical questions. He was not fascinated by the abstract meaning of authority. He wanted to know who carried it, what they were responsible for, and whether they were standing where the responsibility required them to stand.

That instinct became decisive c. Imperial Year 2035.

### The Mornhallow Incident

Halk'en was twenty-two and recently ordained when he was assigned to help with funerary overflow in the market town of Mornhallow. What local clergy believed to be a sequence of individual possessions was actually a distributed spiritual pressure moving through fever patients, attendants, and grieving relatives.

By the time Obsidian arrived, the situation was already collapsing.

A senior specialist was killed. Two priests lost the ability to speak. One hospital ward became so compromised that anyone attempting to enter triggered immediate mimicry from the people inside.

Halk'en was told not to approach the ward.

He approached it anyway.

According to the surviving account, he placed one bare hand on the lintel, another on his own chest, then touched the door and told whatever occupied the ward that the building had been given to the sick and that it had not been given permission to be there.

The voices stopped.

Not permanently. Long enough.

Eleven patients survived the ward.

Witnesses later described a pressure behind Halk'en shaped vaguely like a hand that had not yet decided to become visible. At the time, nobody knew what it meant.

Obsidian removed him from ordinary parish service within the month.

## Entry into Obsidian

Halk'en entered formal Obsidian formation c. Imperial Year 2035 under Rector-Ascendant **Mael Orison**.

He was not an unusually broad candidate. He was poor at radiance projection, merely adequate at formal ward geometry, and uninterested in demonstrating power for its own sake. His strengths were narrower and much more useful: vessel restraint, contested-threshold entry, post-expulsion stabilization, and remaining physically present when other people were becoming afraid of the thing in front of them.

He repeatedly asked for practical containment work rather than prestigious study assignments.

In Imperial Year 2038, during the attempted expulsion of a thirteen-year-old boy named **Anrek Voss**, the red hands became impossible to dismiss as an isolated anomaly.

The possessing entity had learned to exploit the attending clergy's fear of injuring the child. Every attempt at restraint forced Anrek's body into a position where holding him risked breaking him. Halk'en removed his gauntlets and held the boy himself for nine minutes while the other priests prepared another exorcistic sequence.

Five independent witnesses described a large red hand appearing over Halk'en's shoulder and pinning the boy's shadow to the floor.

Anrek survived.

His voice never fully recovered, but he lived long enough to become a hospital copyist. He wrote Halk'en once every year until his death c. Imperial Year 2064.

Halk'en kept all fifty-eight letters.

That case began the monitoring that would follow him for the rest of his life.

### Becoming Rector-Ascendant

Halk'en did not rise quickly through Obsidian because he refused promotion twice.

He argued that his authority was more useful in the field than behind an administrative title. The Church accepted the first refusal and grew considerably less patient with the second.

The matter stopped being optional after the **Red Choir Event** of Imperial Year 2058, when forty-three possessed singers formed a shared vessel network capable of completing one another's evasions whenever the Church attempted ordinary exorcism.

Halk'en entered the ruined nave alone for seven minutes.

When the doors opened, the possessing structure had been forced into a single answerable center. Seventeen choir members were alive. Six were dead. The remaining spiritual structure could finally be severed.

Halk'en was elevated to Rector-Ascendant the following year.

Three Lunar Years later, the Church entrusted him with Sundersaint.

He did not ask for it.

## Practice and Abilities

Halk'en is a **Divine Vessel**: a person whose recursive identity is in active, stabilized relation with divine Presence through the system the Church understands as Divination. Unlike Redactory, which alters reality through directed routing, Divination expresses power through the relationship between a mortal vessel and a divine source.

In Halk'en, that relationship manifests primarily as the **Sevenfold Hand**.

The easiest way to understand the Hand is not as seven separate spells, but as seven progressively deeper forms of making authority real.

At its lower levels, the Hand does not simply overpower things. It establishes whether Halk'en has a right to intervene, preserves testimony, exposes false claims, and makes hostile structures available to lawful address. At its middle levels, it becomes physically obvious: restraining bodies, reinforcing others, identifying the point that must answer, and preventing a possession from hiding behind the person it is using.

At its higher levels, the distinction between spiritual authority and physical force becomes much less comfortable.

The hands can become tangible.

They can seize.

They can command.

At the highest recorded expression, they can make the question of jurisdiction itself stop behaving like a question.

Halk'en's strength therefore does not come primarily from destructive output. He is dangerous because he can take a chaotic situation whose categories are being manipulated—victim or accomplice, invitation or trespass, protection or possession, command or abuse—and force the encounter into a form where something must answer for what it is.

That is also why he is especially effective against possession.

A hostile entity often survives through ambiguity. Halk'en's entire practice is built to reduce ambiguity until the thing in front of him can be restrained, named, commanded, or severed.

## The Sevenfold Hand

Obsidian field records refer to active expression by **hand-count**. The term is practical rather than theological: more hands generally mean greater depth, greater pressure, and much greater risk, but each hand also expresses a different kind of authority rather than merely adding more force.

| Hand | Principle | Practical expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First — Unseen Hand** | Permission | Establishes lawful entrance, detects false authority, and prevents hostile structures from simply declaring themselves outside address. |
| **Second — Veiled Hand** | Witness | Preserves what has been seen, sworn, named, or confessed; makes evasion and concealed claims harder to sustain. |
| **Third — Grasping Hand** | Restraint | Physically seizes, pins, catches, anchors, and holds possessed vessels or hostile manifestations. |
| **Fourth — Open Hand** | Investiture | Temporarily places burden or reinforced authority upon another person who can bear it. |
| **Fifth — Accusing Hand** | Judgment | Identifies the person, claim, object, oath, or structure that must answer before action can proceed cleanly. |
| **Sixth — Hand of Mandate** | Command | Imposes a binding instruction when Halk'en possesses legitimate authority within the situation. |
| **Seventh — Sovereign Palm** | Dominion | Makes the target structurally answerable to authority rather than merely issuing it another command. |

The first two may act so subtly that ordinary witnesses never realize anything happened. The third is the expression most soldiers remember. The sixth is fully tangible. The seventh is rare enough that every confirmed appearance becomes an institutional event in its own right.

Importantly, the Hand is not unrestricted mind control.

Halk'en cannot command arbitrary impossibilities. He cannot make the dead live, make an innocent person guilty, or compel a structure to perform something it has no coherent capacity to perform. His authority must have somewhere legitimate to stand, and the target must possess some actual relation the command can bind.

That limitation is not a technical inconvenience.

It is the moral architecture of the ability.

### Sundersaint, the Parting Mercy

Halk'en's sword is a separate divine artifact rather than another expression of the Sevenfold Hand.

**Sundersaint, the Parting Mercy** predates the Sundering of Selia. Its domain is **Severance**: the pressure by which something already separable can be made to part along the nearest coherent fault-line.

The blade is not a weapon that simply cuts whatever Halk'en wishes hard enough.

It is most effective against things that are falsely joined, parasitically attached, bound beyond right, structurally contested, or already ready to separate. In possession work, this allows it to weaken an entity's hold on a vessel, cut through spiritual bindings, sever imposed compulsions, part hostile rites from the object sustaining them, or exploit an existing flaw in a barrier without necessarily destroying everything around it.

Sundersaint does not decide what deserves to be cut.

Halk'en supplies that judgment.

The pairing is the reason the weapon is so effective and the reason the Church watches it so carefully. The Sevenfold Hand can make a hostile structure answerable. Sundersaint can then part the thing that has been identified as the unlawful bond.

Halk'en's own rule regarding the sword is simple:

> *"The sword parts. It does not save. Do not confuse a clean cut with mercy."*

## Fighting Style

Halk'en fights like a priest who spent centuries learning that the body in front of him may still need to survive the battle.

His preferred sequence is control before destruction.

He establishes jurisdiction, identifies the hostile claim, restrains movement, and narrows the encounter until the dangerous part can be acted upon without treating the entire person, room, structure, or ritual as expendable. Against possessed humans, this matters enormously. Killing the host may end one immediate problem while converting rescue into failure.

The Grasping Hand makes him especially formidable at close range. It can catch weapons, hold thresholds, pin hostile bodies, interrupt motor control, and anchor Halk'en under impact. The Open Hand allows him to reinforce another priest or Votary who is about to fail. The Accusing Hand marks the morally and structurally decisive point of the encounter. The Hand of Mandate is reserved for situations in which direct command has become both legitimate and necessary.

Sundersaint usually comes last.

That is deliberate.

Halk'en does not begin with the cut when the problem might still be restrained, named, confessed, or released.

His armor reflects the same logic. He wears blackened steel plate of deliberately pre-modern form, adjusted and maintained across centuries rather than replaced with contemporary military equipment. Seven red palm-marks are incised across the backplate around the spine. When the Hand manifests visibly, the apparition emerges through those marks as though the armor was built to give the Presence doors.

The result is theatrical despite Halk'en's dislike of theatricality.

He seems faintly annoyed by this fact.

## Limits and Risks

Halk'en's greatest limit is not lack of force. It is the requirement that authority remain legitimate.

The Hand cannot simply transform preference into law. Permission, witness, judgment, and command all depend on a real relation between Halk'en, the target, and the situation. He can exploit that relation with terrifying efficiency, but he cannot invent it without consequence.

The fourth hand carries a separate danger. **Investiture** can temporarily make another person capable of carrying more authority or endurance than they normally possess. That can save a failing exorcism. It can also leave someone permanently changed by the memory of being able to do more than their ordinary self can sustain.

The sword has its own boundaries. Sundersaint cannot erase a demon, destroy divine information, or make an inseparable thing cleanly separable by fiat. It can open the place where expulsion becomes possible. It cannot perform confession, contrition, restoration, or rescue on its own.

The seventh hand is the most serious risk in Halk'en's entire record.

Every confirmed appearance of the Sovereign Palm is followed by extended Ecclesiastic Surgical assessment, repeated confession, and Interior Council review. The concern is not conventional demonic corruption. It is subtler: a mortal who expresses Dominion deeply enough may eventually stop experiencing his own restraint as meaningfully separate from the divine authority acting through him.

Halk'en understands this danger well enough to fear it.

After one of the highest manifestations, he wrote:

> *"The seventh hand is not mine, and therefore I must answer for it more carefully than if it were."*

That sentence is reassuring.

It is also reassuring for precisely the reason that makes the problem terrifying.

He knows the hand may someday feel natural.

## Place in the World

Halk'en is one of Obsidian's most senior living figures and one of the Church's most valuable specialists in possession events that resist ordinary spiritual jurisdiction.

His public reputation is deliberately simpler than his actual status. To most clergy and civilians, he is the Red-Handed Rector: an ancient Obsidian priest, bearer of a dangerous relic sword, and a person whose appearance usually means a situation has become serious enough that normal ecclesiastical procedure has failed.

He is rarely displayed for ceremony.

This is partly Halk'en's preference and partly policy.

The Church does not authorize him for political intimidation, noble disputes, routine heresy investigations, public demonstrations, or relic theater. His abilities are too easily confused with institutional endorsement. A priest capable of making authority physically real cannot be allowed to stand beside every ordinary political command without changing what the command means merely by being present.

That restriction cuts both ways.

Halk'en remains intensely loyal to the Church, but he does not confuse loyalty with comfort. He has publicly opposed coercive formation inside the institution and has little patience for officials who use procedure to avoid responsibility. In one closed consultation concerning institutional hesitation, he reportedly said that measurement had placed itself where judgment should stand and warned the Church not to ask Dominion to bless cowardice by calling it patience.

He believes institutions possess obligations for the same reason people do.

Office must bear its burden.

A Church that commands others while refusing to answer for its own choices would, by Halk'en's theology, be committing the same failure he fears in himself.

## Important Relationships

### Anrek Voss

Anrek Voss was thirteen when a possessing entity learned to use the clergy's fear of hurting him as a weapon. Halk'en's intervention became the first widely witnessed manifestation of the Grasping Hand.

Anrek survived with permanent damage to his voice and later became a hospital copyist.

He wrote Halk'en once each Lunar Year for fifty-eight Lunar Years.

Halk'en kept every letter.

The relationship matters because it places a human life beside the mythology that later grew around the event. For the Church, Anrek's case became evidence of unusual divine expression. For Halk'en, it also remained the life of one boy who had needed to be held without being broken.

That distinction explains a great deal about him.

### Mael Orison

Rector-Ascendant Mael Orison supervised Halk'en's early Obsidian formation and seems to have recognized quickly that the young priest's usefulness did not lie in spectacle. Halk'en was poor at some of the abilities associated with more visibly radiant Church practice and exceptional at the ugly work of restraint, threshold entry, and stabilization.

Orison's willingness to tolerate an unusual candidate gave Halk'en room to become effective without forcing him to imitate a different kind of vessel.

The later dossier gives little detail about their private bond, and anything stronger would be invention. What is clear is that Orison belongs to the small group of people who encountered Halk'en before centuries of office made the Red-Handed Rector larger than the man.

### Primus Caldrin II

Halk'en appears to regard the current Primus with wary respect rather than personal warmth.

Their relationship matters because it exposes Halk'en's understanding of hierarchy. He does not deny the sacred weight of the office. He also does not believe office becomes self-validating merely because the correct person is sitting in the correct chair.

When Caldrin II once asked whether the divine authority acting through Halk'en recognized the papal office, Halk'en answered:

> *"Dominion recognizes office when office bears its burden."*

No one in the room asked him to elaborate.

That was probably wise.

## Restricted Record / Deeper Lore

The public description of Halk'en as an unusually powerful Crowned Divine Vessel is incomplete.

Within restricted Church doctrine, he is one of the **Answered**.

The term does not simply mean that his prayers were answered or that his vessel state is unusually strong. The Answered are rare Crowned vessels whose fusion reaches the **Cynosure**—the central Seraphic source from which an entire constellation of subordinate divine offices proceeds.

Halk'en's Cynosure is **Auctoriel, Seraphim of Dominion**.

He does not contain Auctoriel in full. A mortal identity could not sustain that. Nor is he merely possessed by Auctoriel as though a second person were sitting inside him and issuing instructions. Obsidian's own doctrine treats stable divine Bearing as transformation and integration rather than displacement.

Halk'en remains Halk'en.

That fact is essential because his danger depends on it.

Auctoriel's domain is not domination in the ordinary sense. It is **Dominion**: the principle by which rightful authority becomes binding. Ordination, investiture, lawful restraint, sentence, oath, jurisdiction, office, and the laying-on of hands all fall within its conceptual territory.

The Sevenfold Hand is therefore not a symbolic miracle attached to an unrelated priest.

It is Dominion's contact-state expressed through a man whose entire mortal life had already been organized around the question of when authority has the right to touch another person.

The compatibility is almost too clean.

That is the problem.

Halk'en's greatest protection has always been his insistence that Dominion is not the whole of justice. It is not mercy, truth, healing, potential, obedience, or the Sovereign in full. He has resisted the common failure of severe-domain vessels: allowing the thing their power solves best to become their explanation for every problem.

But Dominion does solve problems.

It enters rooms that refuse intervention. It forces hidden claims into the open. It restrains violence without necessarily killing the victim. It makes hostile structures answer. At seventh-hand depth, it can take a spiritual disaster built around ambiguity and make the ambiguity stop mattering long enough for someone to act.

Four centuries of being proven useful create their own theology.

The Church's deepest concern is therefore not that Halk'en will suddenly become cruel, mad, or possessed.

It is that he may remain faithful, disciplined, compassionate, and convinced that he is merely carrying the burden no one else will—and still become too complete an instrument of the principle inside him.

His blind spot has the same shape as his virtue.

Halk'en understands that burden imposed without rightful permission becomes violence. He does not always understand that some burdens must be put down even when a person once had the strength to carry them. He can recognize forced office as seizure and still struggle to believe peace may itself be a vocation. He can be gentle while coercing. He can fear pride while continuing to command. He can know the seventh hand is not his and still become the person through whom it closes.

This is why the final internal restrictions around him are so severe.

He is not to be used for spectacle or political intimidation. He is not to decide alone that institutional hesitation has become cowardice. He is not to be left without medical and confessional oversight during severe moral disputes involving other Crowned vessels.

And, in the most important version of the rule, he is not to be allowed to decide by himself that the Church has failed so completely that Dominion must act in its place.

Halk'en Orveth is faithful.

He is disciplined.

He is necessary.

None of those words mean safe.
