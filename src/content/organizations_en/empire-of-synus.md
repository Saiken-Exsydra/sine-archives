## A State Built to Contain Power

The **Empire of Synus** is the largest organized state on its stretch of Terra's inhabited band. Roughly four hundred million people live across its central heartland, southern industrial corridor, northern threshold territories, ports, House jurisdictions, military districts, and the long infrastructure lines that connect them.

Synus is an empire, but it is not a simple chain from throne to street. It is a layered political mechanism. The Crown carries sovereignty; the Senate contains national politics; the Courts decide boundaries among authorities; ministries run ordinary administration; the Founding Houses preserve regional stewardship; and the Imperial Military holds armed state power. Alongside them stand institutions that the state needs but does not own outright: the [[apocachynthion|Apocachynthion]], the [[the-church|Church]], and [[sine|SiNE]].

No one part is permitted to become the whole Empire. That is the design.

## Selia and the Founding Settlement

Before Synus, the region was ruled by twelve Redactor dynasties whose bloodlines joined land, political office, metaphysical practice, and inherited authority. Their attempt at the **Rite of Selia** ended in the Sundering of Year 1025: mass death, broken lineages, damaged terrain, institutional collapse, and proof that no power capable of reaching so deeply could be trusted to regulate itself.

The settlement of **Year 1026** preserved what the devastated region still needed while placing limits around it. Twelve recoverable old Houses entered the new compact. Three commercial Houses whose credit, extraction, and transport networks had made reconstruction possible were elevated beside them. Together they became the [[imperial-houses|Fifteen Founding Houses]]. House Faldryn received the Green Crown, but the new sovereign was never granted the unchecked authority of the vanished kingdom.

The Empire was founded through relief work, legal fear, sacred sanction, surviving lineage, and material necessity. Its first principle was not purity or unity. It was containment.

## How Authority Is Divided

The **Green Crown** is the sovereign center and guarantor of national continuity. It appoints government, commands the Imperial Military through law, represents Synus abroad, and activates bounded emergency powers. It cannot simply dissolve every compact or convert technical necessity into personal rule.

The **Senate** gives the Founding Houses, regions, cities, and recognized institutions a chamber in which competing interests can be made visible without becoming private war. The **Imperial Courts** decide where one lawful authority stops and another begins. The ministries administer taxation, health, transport, industry, agriculture, public safety, foreign commerce, and military readiness.

This division becomes most visible during a crisis. Civil movement and casualty accounting remain civil responsibilities. The military directs war. The Apocachynthion governs Redactory practice. The Church governs protected rites and its own vessels. SiNE controls the technical act inside its infrastructure. Emergency law coordinates those powers; it does not erase them.

## The Empire Is Not SiNE

Synus depends on SiNE for BrightCrystal refinement, grid stability, strategic transport, industrial standards, and contracted force. That dependence is profound enough to shape public credit and national survival. It still does not make the Wonderwall Company the state.

SiNE cannot crown an Empress, issue sovereign currency, command the Courts, own registered Redactors, or turn a corporate refusal into imperial law. The Empire, in turn, cannot treat every SiNE system as ordinary public property without risking the infrastructure it is trying to protect. Their relationship is one of constitutional codependence, not hidden corporate ownership.

## A Continent of Uneven Settlement

Most imperial citizens live in dense cities, river corridors, ports, rail nodes, and industrial belts. Vast areas between them are forest, plateau, agricultural reserve, extraction territory, sealed ruin, military buffer, Bright-Side transition, or legally claimed land with little permanent settlement. This is why House stewardship, municipal government, ministry administration, and compact institutions overlap instead of replacing one another.

At ordinary scale, the Empire is experienced through schools, trams, clinics, warning systems, work licenses, courts, taxes, pensions, and queues. Its constitutional machinery matters because those daily systems must keep working through [[callings|Callings]], war, Redactory incidents, industrial failures, and the enormous distances between one populated corridor and the next.

Synus calls that continuity civilization. Its institutions remember what happened when continuity failed.
