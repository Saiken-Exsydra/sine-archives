## Not Fifteen Little States

The **Founding Houses** are hereditary institutions inside the [[empire-of-synus|Empire of Synus]]. They are not independent countries, do not own the people living on their land, cannot conduct foreign policy, and cannot place House law above the Crown, Courts, Senate, or ministries.

They are also not decorative aristocracy.

Synus stretches across a vast and unevenly settled territory. Many of its most important roads, mines, ports, forests, hospitals, archives, military approaches, and industrial districts lie far from dense ministry administration. The Empire retained the Houses because they preserve local knowledge and continuity that cannot be replaced quickly from Kalt'sen. It bound them because the old dynastic order proved what hereditary power becomes when no authority can refuse it.

## House Jurisdiction

**House Jurisdiction** is delegated, hereditary, compact-bound authority. Depending on the House and region, it may include estates, local patronage, archive custody, stewardship of roads or ports, charity and relief obligations, industrial compacts, internal family courts, or appointment rights to advisory bodies.

It does not permit a House to block Redactor registration, obstruct imperial tax, maintain a private army, override emergency law, interfere with protected institutional authority, or claim a resident by blood, employment, or geography. A House governor is a recognized stewardship officer, not a provincial sovereign.

House culture can expect a Redactor to learn personal and household defense through Academy formation and lawful drills, but defensive competence is neither hereditary power nor a license for private war: guards, retainers, and premises remain bounded by civil, Apocachynthion, and battlefield jurisdiction.

Citizens usually encounter House Jurisdiction through practical questions: who built the bridge, sponsors the scholarship, owns the foundry, maintains the harbor, funds the hospital, pays the death stipend, or keeps the old archive closed.

## The Fifteen

The founding compact joined three groups.

The three **Refuser Houses** declined the Rite of Selia:

- **Faldryn** — the Green Crown and imperial legitimacy;
- **Wonderwall** — material continuity, BrightCrystal infrastructure, and SiNE;
- **Vesperath** — sacred legitimacy and the Church compact, rechartered from Whitechurch.

Nine other old dynasties survived Selia in broken or reconstituted form:

- **Karsenne** — Redactor archives and Apocachynthion patronage;
- **Veyrholt** — courts, seals, and compact law;
- **Merovain** — civic order and public administration;
- **Rauthen** — military roads and fortress logistics;
- **Engelmeyer** — foundries and war industry;
- **Aldervenn** — northern ports and naval continuity;
- **Drauker** — threshold settlements and Night Legion service;
- **Elsenvar** — agriculture, settlement, and food security;
- **Maroven** — hospitals, households, and recovery.

Three commercial Houses were elevated because reconstruction could not proceed without them:

- **Aurivane** — credit and bond finance;
- **Dalkenrath** — mines, quarries, and extraction;
- **Valtrehn** — roads, bridges, freight, and public works.

Each holds one inherited answer to a problem the Empire has never managed to centralize safely.

## Houses Beyond the Fifteen

Synus also recognizes service Houses and cadet dignities without granting them a Founding Senate seat. **House Northgrave** carries Night Legion memory, veteran households, and the roll of northern settlements abandoned to preserve the line. **House Coldwater** preserves convoy, evacuation, and ice-navigation practice associated with the sovereign Imperial Cold Fleet.

Neither replaces its parent tradition or inherits automatic command. Service can create dignity. It does not manufacture sovereignty.

## Why the Arrangement Endures

House power produces loyalty, access, protection, patronage, resentment, and inequality in the same communities. The system is deliberately imperfect. Absolute centralization would abandon local capacity; renewed dynastic sovereignty would risk rebuilding the order that produced Selia.

The imperial settlement keeps the Houses close enough to serve and bounded enough to survive.
