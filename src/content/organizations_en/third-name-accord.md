The **Third Name Accord** is the Empire's governing settlement for people whose continuity includes fusion, inherited memory, reconstructed identity, or another condition that makes ordinary lineage law inadequate.

It is named for [[alithia-wonderwall|Al'ithia Rae Wonderwall]], but it is not a family privilege. The Accord begins from a simple principle: a living person is not reducible to the people, institutions, memories, or methods from which that person emerged.

## The Crisis of Imperial Years 2191–2192

The settlement followed a protected continuity case in which sealed evidence showed that an institution had attempted to make living people serve an assigned historical purpose. The public record establishes the abuse and the legal remedy without publishing the protected persons, methods, or complete evidentiary record.

Al'ithia's petition placed a related question before the Court, Senate, Crown, Church, Apocachynthion, Wonderwall, and SiNE: could the person presently standing before the law be recognized without being converted into a predecessor's office, property, marriage, or function?

The answer was yes.

## One Present Person

The Accord recognizes Al'ithia as a stable **third self**. Her continuity with Athena Rae and the A'licia-linked First Radiance is legally relevant, but she is identical to neither and cannot be jointly owned through either predecessor's estate.

Inherited memory may be evidence. It is not automatic entitlement. A present claimant does not resume a predecessor's marriage, title, contract, debt, property, or political office merely by remembering it. Such relationships continue only through the recognition and lawful choice of living people.

## No Ownership and No Assigned Continuity

No institution may own, patent, assign, or reproduce a person as a continuity asset. A dead founder, ruler, saint, strategist, or House head cannot resume office through memory, fusion, resemblance, or reconstruction alone.

The Accord does not erase complicated histories. It establishes the boundary that matters most: continuity may explain a person's circumstances, but it cannot decide that person's purpose.
